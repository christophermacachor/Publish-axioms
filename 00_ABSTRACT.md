## Abstract

Contemporary physics operates on vector, tensor, spinor, and matrix formalisms that treat directional quantities as ontologically fundamental. This paper proposes an alternative axiomatic framework grounded in scalar field primacy: the hypothesis that reality at its substrate level is described entirely by scalar fields ψ: D_P → ℝ, with all directional quantities emerging from scalar gradients, curvatures, and density differentials.

We present the Macachor Scalar Theorem, which generalizes the classical Pythagorean theorem to n-dimensional orthogonal scalar superposition:

ρ²_total = Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ ρᵢ²

where ρᵢ represents the scalar density of the i-th orthogonal component. The theorem is proven by induction, with the base case (n = 2) corresponding to the classical a² + b² = c² relation, verified through 93 distinct geometric, algebraic, and analytic proofs. The inductive step demonstrates that orthogonality preservation under composition extends the Pythagorean identity to arbitrary dimensionality.

A quaternion extension is derived, establishing that the four-square identity |p·q|² = |p|²·|q|² conserves scalar density under multiplicative composition in four-dimensional scalar space. This provides a norm-preserving framework for quantum operations, where any unitary transformation must preserve the quaternion norm ||q||² = 1, ensuring quantum states remain on the scalar density equilibrium manifold.

The framework introduces the Macachor Absolute scalar magnitude ℳ = (√5 − 1)/2 ≈ 0.618 as a fundamental coherence constant governing equilibrium ratios, quantization boundaries, and eigenmode selection. For the special case where two orthogonal components each carry density ℳ, the total coherence scalar becomes ρ_total = √2 · ℳ = √((3 − √5)/2) ≈ 0.874, establishing a hardware coherence bound designated the "Golden Scalar Lock."

Two operational architectures are formalized: (1) KIWF (Knowledge → Intelligence → Wisdom → Focus), an information density pipeline that filters scalar data through coherence gates; and (2) CRHA (Coherence → Resonance → Harmony → Alignment), a standing-wave coherence architecture for physical substrate design. The united equation Ψ²_MSOS = ρ²_KIWF + ρ²_CRHA yields the total system coherence scalar Ψ_MSOS = √2 · ℳ.

Physical interpretations are provided for De Pretto's ether hypothesis (1903–1904), Newton's corpuscular optics (1704), and Kirchauer's scalar diffraction formalism (1998), demonstrating that historical scalar substrate theories are consistent with the framework and not superseded by vector formalisms but rather subsumed as special cases.

Applications to quantum hardware coherence protocols and scalar aircraft flux-pinning architecture are presented, with the condition a² + b² = c² (where a = magnetic scalar potential, b = gas/plasma density differential, c = resultant flux magnitude) ensuring that vector directional changes preserve scalar density conservation.

The framework is supported by ten formal corollaries and lemmas covering scalar gradient emergence, orthogonality preservation, coherence decay at boundaries, density differential continuity, n-component golden locks, scalar uncertainty relations, aperture transmission coefficients, quaternion coherence manifolds, flux-pinning stability bounds, and scalar entropy bounds.

## Plain Language Summary

This work proposes that the fundamental building blocks of physical reality are scalar fields — quantities described by single numbers at each point in space and time — rather than vectors or tensors with directional components. The classical Pythagorean theorem (a² + b² = c²) is shown to be a special case of a universal scalar superposition law that applies to any number of dimensions. The framework extends into quaternion (4D) algebra, providing a mathematical foundation for quantum computing hardware that preserves coherence during operations. A fundamental constant ℳ = (√5 − 1)/2 is identified as governing all stable configurations, with applications to quantum hardware design, aircraft propulsion via flux pinning, and information system architecture.
